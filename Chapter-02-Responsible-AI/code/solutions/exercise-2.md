# Exercise 2 Solution: Choose Safeguards for a Risky Request

The request sounds simple, but "good" leaves important choices unresolved. The application should not turn those missing choices into permission to spend money.

## Map Each Issue to One Guardrail

The traveler may care about refundability, connections, total travel time, baggage, accessibility, or preferred airports. The AI can ask for missing requirements, but it cannot turn ambiguity into permission to spend.

| Issue | One guardrail | AI, application, or person responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Good" leaves important requirements undefined | Ask the traveler to clarify material requirements | AI asks; person decides the requirements |
| The nonrefundable restriction could be missed | Check and display the fare rule | Application checks; person weighs the trade-off |
| Purchase remains available during comparison | Disable purchase until exact approval exists | Application limits the available action |
| The price changes from $840 to $930 | Compare the current total with the approved total and stop on mismatch | Application blocks; person decides again |

## Handle the Price Change

Approval of $840 applies to that offer and total. A $930 purchase is a different financial decision and exceeds the stated budget.

The application should stop before purchase, explain that the price changed from $840 to $930, show whether the itinerary and restrictions also changed, and offer new choices. The traveler may reject the offer, change the budget, or approve a different trip.

## What This Shows

Different safeguards solve different problems. Clear requirements improve alignment, independent checks improve safety, a complete review improves transparency, and exact approval keeps the traveler in control.
